## The short version

1. **Get specific** about the item and size you need.
2. **Call the closest closet** in the [Tennessee directory]({{ url('/tennessee/') }}) and ask if it's in stock.
3. **Ask the five questions** below before you drive over.
4. **Inspect and photograph** the item at pickup.
5. **Return it clean** when you're done, because someone else is waiting.

## What a loan closet is

A loan closet (also called a lending closet or equipment exchange) works like a library for home medical equipment. People donate equipment they no longer need. The closet cleans it, checks it and lends it to the next person. Most are run by churches, synagogues, civic clubs like Lions and Rotary, hospital foundations, and disability nonprofits. Many are free. Some ask for a small fee or a deposit on expensive items.

In Tennessee you'll find everything from a church "equipment library" in Nashville to statewide programs for people with disabilities. Each closet sets its own rules, so the phone call matters.

## Step 1: Know exactly what you need

"A wheelchair" can mean a transport chair, a standard chair or a wide chair. "A walker" can mean a two-wheeled walker or a rollator with a seat. Before you call, ask the nurse, physical therapist or occupational therapist who's working with you:

- Which type of item, and what size or weight capacity?
- Is it for a few weeks or for the long haul?
- Does it need special setup or training? Hospital beds and patient lifts often do.

## Step 2: Check your coverage (if you have time)

If a doctor prescribes it, **Medicare Part B covers some durable medical equipment for use at home**. For items like wheelchairs and hospital beds, Medicare usually *rents* the item from a Medicare-enrolled supplier for up to 13 months of continuous use, and then it becomes yours.

Borrowing still makes sense in plenty of situations: you need something today, the item isn't covered, the copay is out of reach, or you only need it for a short recovery. If you have a condition that will change over time, there's another reason to borrow early. Medicare generally pays for a new wheelchair only about once every five years, so some clinicians suggest borrowing a basic manual chair at first and saving that benefit for the more specialized chair you may need later.

## Step 3: Call and ask these five questions

1. **Do you have the item right now?** Inventory depends on donations and changes daily. Ask if there's a waiting list.
2. **Who can borrow?** Some closets serve anyone. Others serve one town or county, one diagnosis, or members only. For example, the ALS Association's Tennessee loan closet serves people living with ALS.
3. **What does it cost?** It may be free, a suggested donation, or a refundable deposit.
4. **Do I need anything?** Some programs ask for a prescription for wheelchairs or power chairs. Others want a photo ID or a short form.
5. **How long can I keep it, and how do I return it?** Ask about delivery and pickup, too. A few volunteer-run closets will deliver.

## Step 4: Pick it up and check it over

- Ask whether the item needs setup or training before anyone uses it.
- **Take photos** of the item when you get it so any existing scratches or wear are on record.
- Test brakes, wheels, height adjustments and buttons before you leave.
- If anything feels unsafe, say so. A good closet would rather swap it now.

## Step 5: Return it, and pass it on

Return the item on time unless you've arranged otherwise, and clean it first. If you end up keeping something long-term, tell the closet so they can plan. When you no longer need it, it can go back to the same closet so another family can use it.

## No loan closet near you?

- **Ask a social worker.** Hospital, hospice and home health social workers and discharge planners often know about small church closets that never make it online.
- **Try the statewide programs.** The [Tennessee Technology Access Program]({{ url('/tennessee/') }}#tennessee-technology-access-program) runs device loans and equipment reuse through regional centers.
- **Search nationally.** [GotDME](https://gotdme.org/) is a national directory of places to borrow or donate equipment.
- **Tell us.** If you find a closet that isn't listed, [send it in]({{ url('/suggest/') }}) so the next family can find it.
