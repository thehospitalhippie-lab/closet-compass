What to do with medical equipment after someone dies

The hospital bed, the wheelchair, the shower chair. When someone dies at home, the equipment stays behind. Here's how to sort what goes back, what's yours, and who in Tennessee can use it next.

Updated Nov 5, 2026 · Researched by Yeha, an AI assistant, from the sources listed below

First, there’s no rush. Nothing here has to happen today. When you’re ready, sort the equipment into three piles: goes back, yours to give, and not sure.

Goes back: equipment from hospice

If your person was on hospice, the hospice often supplied the big items, like the hospital bed, oxygen, wheelchair and bedside commode. Hospice staff arrange removal of equipment the hospice supplied. Call the hospice’s main number and they’ll schedule a pickup. Don’t donate these items, because they belong to the hospice or its equipment company.

Hospice staff also handle disposal of medications, which federal rules require. And hospice grief support doesn’t end at the death: families can usually get bereavement support for up to a year afterward.

Goes back (maybe): Medicare rentals

Outside of hospice, Medicare usually rents items like hospital beds and wheelchairs from a supplier for up to 13 months of continuous use. After that, ownership passes to the patient. Oxygen equipment follows a longer 36-month rule.

So:

  • Rented for less than 13 months? It likely still belongs to the supplier. Call the company named on the equipment’s label or on the billing statements.
  • Rented for 13 months or more, or bought outright? It’s probably yours to donate.
  • Not sure? Call the supplier and ask. It takes one phone call to avoid giving away something that isn’t yours.

Yours to give: where to donate in Tennessee

These programs in the directory accept donated equipment. Call first to check what they need right now:

  • Roan Creek Baptist Church — Medical Equipment Ministry — Mountain City. Contact: 423-727-7061
  • Spark (formerly ETTAC) — Mobility & Medical Equipment — Knoxville. Contact: 865-219-0130
  • Goodwill of the Greater Chattanooga Area — HELPs Equipment Program — Chattanooga. Contact: 423-629-2501 (Opportunity Campus)
  • Mid-South Access Center for Technology (University of Memphis) — Memphis. Contact: 901-678-1489
  • STAR Center — Durable Medical Equipment — Jackson. Contact: 731-554-5105
  • ALS Association Tennessee — Equipment Loan Closet — Statewide (Nashville-based chapter).
  • Tennessee Technology Access Program (TTAP) — Device Loan & Reuse — Statewide (Nashville office). Contact: 1-833-772-8347 (One DHS Contact Center)

Don’t see one near you? The full directory lists more places, and many church closets take donations too. Just ask.

Before you drop it off

  • Call ahead. Closets have limited space. Ask what they need and whether they can pick up large items like beds or lift chairs.
  • Clean it. Wipe it down and include chargers, manuals and any small parts.
  • Ask about oxygen and breathing equipment first. Some lending programs can’t accept respiratory equipment. The ALS Association’s Florida program, for example, says it can’t house oxygen equipment.
  • Unopened supplies may help. Some programs hand out supplies as well as equipment. Spark in Knoxville, for one, lists incontinence products among what it gives out. Ask before bringing opened packages.
  • Ask for a receipt if you’ll want one for taxes.

If you’re reading this after a recent loss, we’re sorry. Passing a wheelchair or a bed on to someone who needs it is a small, real kindness, and it can wait until you’re ready.
